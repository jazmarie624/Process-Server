English — the lines

Clean, no commentary. Read these out loud in the car until they come without thinking.

1 · Correct person answers
Confirm
Good morning. Are you Maria Ramirez?
Identify
My name is Jazmin Aguayo. I'm a certified process server with the Ninth Judicial Circuit.
Deliver
I have legal documents for you from the Osceola County court. These are for you.
Close
You've been served. The papers explain what this is about and the deadline to respond. Have a good day.
2 · Someone else answers — substitute service
Abode
Hi, my name is Jazmin Aguayo. Does Maria Ramirez live here?
Resident + age
And do you live here too? Are you fifteen or older?
Inform of contents
I'm leaving these with you. They are legal documents for Maria — a summons and complaint from the court. Please give them to her.
Get the name
Can I get your first name and your relationship to her? It goes on the court's form.
3 · "What is this about?"
Answer
I'm not able to give legal advice and I don't know the details of the case. The first page has the case number and the court. The attorney's name and phone number are on the front, or you can call the clerk of court.
4 · They refuse to take the papers
After identity is confirmed
That's completely fine. I'm placing them right here. You have been served.
5 · They won't open but you can hear them
Through the door
Miss Ramirez, my name is Jazmin Aguayo. I'm a certified process server. I have court documents for you. I'm going to leave them at your door.
6 · Gate guard or front desk
To the guard
Good morning. My name is Jazmin Aguayo, I'm a certified process server for the Ninth Judicial Circuit — here's my ID and my server number. Florida Statute 48.031(7) requires a gated community to grant unannounced entry for service of process. I'm not asking you to call ahead. I just need the gate.
7 · Serving at a workplace
To the manager
Hi, my name is Jazmin Aguayo and I'm a certified process server. I need to serve one of your employees with legal documents. Florida law requires you to let me do that in a private area — and I'd rather do it discreetly than in front of customers. Where would you like to put us?
8 · Hostile or yelling
Flat and calm, already stepping back
I'm not with the police and I'm not here to argue. My name is Jazmin Aguayo and I'm a certified process server. I don't know anything about your case — I just deliver the documents. Everything you need is inside. Have a good day.
9 · They're evasive about who they are
Wrong-detail method
I have a delivery for a Maria Ramirez with a middle initial J — is that you, or do I have the wrong unit?
Spelling method
Can you spell the last name for me so I mark this correctly?

English — with delivery notes

Same lines, plus how to say them and what happens if you get it wrong.

The 10-second reset before every knock. Papers folded low in your non-dominant hand. ID visible. Phone recording or timestamping. Stand to the hinge side, never centered. Read the name once more so you say it right the first time. Three firm knocks plus the doorbell, then one step back.
1 · Correct person answers
Confirm — warm, quick, no hesitation
Good morning. Are you Maria Ramirez?
Use their name in the first three seconds. Hesitation reads as a threat; confidence reads as routine. Do not hand over anything until they answer.
Identify — steady, unhurried
My name is Jazmin Aguayo. I'm a certified process server with the Ninth Judicial Circuit.
Say certified process server, never anything that could sound like law enforcement. Implying you're a cop is a crime in Florida and it voids the service.
Deliver — matter of fact
I have legal documents for you from the Osceola County court. These are for you.
Extend the papers as you say it. Most people take them automatically.
Close — then leave
You've been served. The papers explain what this is about and the deadline to respond. Have a good day.
Turn and go. Don't linger, don't explain, don't answer legal questions. Note the exact time before you reach your car — memory degrades within minutes.
2 · Someone else answers — substitute service
Prong one — is this the abode?
Hi, my name is Jazmin Aguayo. Does Maria Ramirez live here?
"Usual place of abode" means where she actually lives right now. Not a mailing address, not last month's apartment.
Prong two — the one people skip
And do you live here too? Are you fifteen or older?
They must reside there. A visiting relative, a babysitter, a contractor, a girlfriend who stays over — none of those count. Florida courts have quashed service over exactly this. Ask, and write down what they said.
Prong three — say it out loud
I'm leaving these with you. They are legal documents for Maria — a summons and complaint from the court. Please give them to her.
Informing them of the contents is a spoken act you have to actually perform and then swear to on the return. Never skip it because it feels obvious.
For your return
Can I get your first name and your relationship to her? It goes on the court's form.
You need the name, the relationship, and their approximate age for the return.
If they say they don't live there — stop. Do not leave the papers. That's a defective serve. Log it as an attempt, note who you spoke to and exactly what they said, and come back at a different hour.
3 · "What is this about?"
Answer once, then leave
I'm not able to give legal advice and I don't know the details of the case. The first page has the case number and the court. The attorney's name and phone number are on the front, or you can call the clerk of court.
Never speculate. "It looks like a credit card thing" is legal advice from a stranger and it can end up in a deposition. Handing them a next step is also what calms people down.
4 · They refuse to take the papers
Only after identity is confirmed
That's completely fine. I'm placing them right here. You have been served.
Once you've identified the right person and told them what the papers are, refusal doesn't defeat service. Set them down in near proximity — at their feet, on the threshold, tucked in the door. On the return, write that you identified them, informed them, they refused, and you left the documents in their presence.
Never do this without confirmed identity. Papers left with a stranger who refused to say who they are is not service.
5 · They won't open but you can hear them
Through the door
Miss Ramirez, my name is Jazmin Aguayo. I'm a certified process server. I have court documents for you. I'm going to leave them at your door.
Say it once, clearly, loud enough to be heard.
This is usually an attempt, not a serve. A voice through a door is not confirmed identity. Unless you saw them, or they answered to their name and you can swear to who they are, log it as an attempt and come back at a different hour. This is exactly where new servers get burned.
6 · Gate guard or front desk
Calm, credentialed, not asking permission
Good morning. My name is Jazmin Aguayo, I'm a certified process server for the Ninth Judicial Circuit — here's my ID and my server number. Florida Statute 48.031(7) requires a gated community to grant unannounced entry for service of process. I'm not asking you to call ahead. I just need the gate.
Keep the statute printed in your door bag and hand it over instead of arguing. If they still refuse: get the guard's name, the company name, and the time, then tell the attorney. That's their motion to make, not your fight. Never force a gate.
7 · Serving at a workplace
Frame it as protecting them
Hi, my name is Jazmin Aguayo and I'm a certified process server. I need to serve one of your employees with legal documents. Florida law requires you to let me do that in a private area — and I'd rather do it discreetly than in front of customers. Where would you like to put us?
Offering to avoid a scene gets you a back office almost every time. The statute — employers must provide a private area, with a fine up to $1,000 for refusing — is your backup, not your opener.
8 · Hostile or yelling
Lower your voice, step back as you speak
I'm not with the police and I'm not here to argue. My name is Jazmin Aguayo and I'm a certified process server. I don't know anything about your case — I just deliver the documents. Everything you need is inside. Have a good day.
People match volume. Going quieter pulls them down with you. Say it once, then leave — two steps backward, turn, go. You don't get a second serve if you're injured.
9 · They're evasive about who they are
Wrong-detail method
I have a delivery for a Maria Ramirez with a middle initial J — is that you, or do I have the wrong unit?
People correct wrong information almost reflexively.
Spelling method
Can you spell the last name for me so I mark this correctly?
The line you don't cross. Confirming identity is fine. Pretending to be UPS, a florist, a utility worker, or law enforcement is not — it can void your service, cost your certification, and impersonating police is a crime. Be creative about timing, never about who you are.

Español — las líneas (Spanish — the lines)

Spanish first, English meaning underneath in parentheses. Always usted, never tú — formality is protection.

1 · La persona correcta abre la puerta (The right person answers the door)
Confirmar (Confirm who they are)
Buenos días. ¿Es usted María Ramírez?
(Good morning. Are you Maria Ramirez?)
Identificarse (Identify yourself)
Mi nombre es Jazmin Aguayo. Soy servidora de procesos certificada del Noveno Circuito Judicial.
(My name is Jazmin Aguayo. I am a certified process server with the Ninth Judicial Circuit.)
Entregar (Hand over the papers)
Tengo documentos legales para usted del tribunal del condado de Osceola. Estos son para usted.
(I have legal documents for you from the Osceola County court. These are for you.)
Cerrar (Close and leave)
Ha sido notificada oficialmente. Los papeles explican de qué se trata y la fecha límite para responder. Que tenga buen día.
(You have been served. The papers explain what this is about and the deadline to respond. Have a good day.)
2 · Otra persona abre — entrega sustituta (Someone else answers — substitute service)
El domicilio (Is this the abode?)
Hola, mi nombre es Jazmin Aguayo. ¿Vive aquí María Ramírez?
(Hi, my name is Jazmin Aguayo. Does Maria Ramirez live here?)
Residencia y edad (Do they reside there, and are they 15+?)
¿Y usted también vive aquí? ¿Tiene quince años o más?
(And do you live here too? Are you fifteen or older?)
Informar del contenido (Inform them of the contents)
Le voy a dejar estos documentos. Son documentos legales para María — una citación y una demanda del tribunal. Por favor entrégueselos a ella.
(I am leaving these documents with you. They are legal documents for Maria — a summons and a complaint from the court. Please give them to her.)
Para el formulario (For your return of service)
¿Me puede dar su nombre y su relación con ella? Es para el formulario del tribunal.
(Can I get your name and your relationship to her? It is for the court’s form.)
3 · "¿De qué se trata esto?" ("What is this about?")
Respuesta (Your answer)
No puedo dar consejos legales y no conozco los detalles del caso. La primera página tiene el número del caso y el tribunal. El nombre y el teléfono del abogado están en la primera página, o puede llamar a la secretaría del tribunal.
(I cannot give legal advice and I do not know the details of the case. The first page has the case number and the court. The attorney’s name and phone number are on the first page, or you can call the clerk of court.)
4 · Se niega a recibir los documentos (They refuse to take the papers)
Después de confirmar la identidad (Only after identity is confirmed)
Está bien, no hay problema. Los dejo aquí mismo. Usted ha sido notificado oficialmente.
(That is fine, no problem. I am leaving them right here. You have been served.)
5 · No abre la puerta pero usted la escucha (They will not open the door but you can hear them)
A través de la puerta (Through the door)
Señora Ramírez, mi nombre es Jazmin Aguayo. Soy servidora de procesos certificada. Tengo documentos del tribunal para usted. Los voy a dejar en su puerta.
(Ms. Ramirez, my name is Jazmin Aguayo. I am a certified process server. I have court documents for you. I am going to leave them at your door.)
6 · Guardia del portón o recepción (Gate guard or front desk)
Al guardia (To the guard)
Buenos días. Mi nombre es Jazmin Aguayo, soy servidora de procesos certificada del Noveno Circuito Judicial — aquí está mi identificación y mi número. El Estatuto de Florida 48.031(7) requiere que una comunidad con portón permita la entrada sin anunciar para la entrega de documentos legales. No le pido que llame. Solo necesito que abra el portón.
(Good morning. My name is Jazmin Aguayo, I am a certified process server for the Ninth Judicial Circuit — here is my ID and my server number. Florida Statute 48.031(7) requires a gated community to grant unannounced entry for service of process. I am not asking you to call ahead. I just need you to open the gate.)
7 · Entrega en el lugar de trabajo (Serving at a workplace)
Al gerente (To the manager)
Hola, mi nombre es Jazmin Aguayo y soy servidora de procesos certificada. Necesito entregarle documentos legales a uno de sus empleados. La ley de Florida requiere que me permita hacerlo en un área privada — y prefiero hacerlo con discreción, no frente a los clientes. ¿Dónde prefiere que lo hagamos?
(Hi, my name is Jazmin Aguayo and I am a certified process server. I need to serve legal documents to one of your employees. Florida law requires you to let me do that in a private area — and I would rather do it discreetly, not in front of customers. Where would you prefer we do this?)
8 · Persona hostil o gritando (Hostile or yelling)
Calmada, ya retrocediendo (Calm, already stepping back)
No soy policía y no vine a discutir. Mi nombre es Jazmin Aguayo y soy servidora de procesos certificada. No sé nada de su caso — solo entrego los documentos. Toda la información está adentro. Que tenga buen día.
(I am not with the police and I did not come to argue. My name is Jazmin Aguayo and I am a certified process server. I do not know anything about your case — I only deliver the documents. All the information is inside. Have a good day.)
9 · Evade decir quién es (They are evasive about who they are)
Método del dato incorrecto (The wrong-detail method)
Tengo una entrega para María Ramírez con la inicial J — ¿es usted, o tengo la unidad equivocada?
(I have a delivery for Maria Ramirez with a middle initial J — is that you, or do I have the wrong unit?)
Método de deletrear (The spelling method)
¿Me puede deletrear el apellido para marcarlo correctamente?
(Can you spell the last name for me so I mark this correctly?)
Si se le acaba el español (If your Spanish runs out): "Disculpe, mi español es limitado — toda la información está en los papeles."
(Excuse me, my Spanish is limited — all the information is in the papers.) Honest beats fluent, every time.
