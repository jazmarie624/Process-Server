The daily rhythm

1 · The Night Before

15–20 min · evening
Tomorrow is won tonight. Never build a route in the morning.
  • Pull tomorrow's jobs from the accordion, sorted by serve-by dateAnything due inside 5 days goes in the morning prime block
  • Print and assemble each packet — summons plus complaint, two copies of everythingA summons without the complaint is a defective serve
  • Pre-stamp every packet with your name and CPS numberOnly date, time, and initials get handwritten at the door
  • Pre-fill each return of service — everything except date, time, and manner
  • Sticky note on each packet: physical description, vehicle, best time to catch them
  • Highlight the deadline in yellow on the outside of the packet
  • Build the route — batch by zone, 6–8 stops, hardest defendant first, one flex slot
  • Check for stacked addresses — same complex or same community across different jobs
  • Load the accordion in route order, next stop on top
  • Charge everything — phone, battery pack, body cam
  • Fill the tank and refill the water
  • Text your route window to your person, live location on
Never on the list: Sunday serves. Service on Sunday is void and you're personally liable. Sunday is for scouting and the weekly reset only.

2 · Car Load-Out

Tape inside the glovebox
Eight zones. If it doesn't have a zone, it doesn't come in the car.
  • Dash — two phone mounts, one navigating, one recording
  • Passenger seat — clipboard box, TODAY only, in route order
  • Passenger footwell — accordion file, every open job, tabbed by due date
  • Center console — pens, self-inking stamp, highlighter, sticky notes, $20 cash
  • Glovebox — certification copy, ID card backup, bond copy, printed §48.031(7), blank returns
  • Trunk — cooler, water, cold towel, spare closed-toe shoes, umbrella, gallon bags, bug spray, first aid
  • Driver door — battery pack and cable, hand sanitizer, sunglasses
  • Back seat — EMPTY. This is a rule, not a suggestionThe moment papers live in the back seat, the system is gone
On your person, every door: ID visible on a lanyard, papers folded low in your non-dominant hand, phone recording or timestamping, dominant hand free.

3 · At the Door

Per serve · pocket card
Ten seconds of scanning, then knock.
  • Scan the house — AC running, mail piled, trash cans, tags, blinds, toys, dog, camera
  • Photograph every vehicle tag at the address
  • Park nose-out, never blocking a driveway, two houses down if possible
  • Stand to the hinge side, one step back. Three firm knocks plus the doorbell
  • Confirm identity before you hand anything over
  • If substitute: confirm it's the abode → confirm they live there and are 15+ → say the contents out loudAll three prongs or the service can be quashed
  • Get the name and relationship of whoever you served
  • Write date, time, initials, and your CPS number on the first page of the process
  • Leave immediately. No explaining, no legal questions, no lingering
  • Note the exact time before you reach the car
Abort and log the attempt instead if: loose dog, no confirmed identity through a closed door, the person says they don't live there, or anything feels wrong. An aborted attempt is still billable and documented.

4 · After Each Serve — In the Car

2 min · before you pull off
Not tonight. Not at the next stop. Right now, engine running.
  • Exact time written down, to the minute
  • Photo taken — house number and front door, timestamp and GPS embedded
  • Voice-memo your observations while they're freshVehicles, lights, who answered, what they said, what you saw
  • Log the attempt in the app — verify the GPS actually captured
  • Complete the return — manner, name served, capacity, documents list
  • Text the client: served, time, return tonight
  • Move the packet out of the active clipboard into the done side
  • Flag the address if anything went badly — never return there alone

5 · Daily Close-Out

15 min · every working night
This is the job. Servers don't lose clients because they can't serve — they lose them because nobody can get a status update.
  • Every attempt logged, including the no-answers, with exact times
  • Photos uploaded to the right case folder CASE#_LASTNAME — same day, every day
  • Returns signed and sent for everything served today
  • Run the affidavit audit on every return before it goes out (sheet 6)
  • Invoice everything served today, return attached
  • Flag at-risk jobs — deadline inside 5 days with no successful attempt gets an email tonight
  • Empty the inbox bin — every new job routed into the accordion by due date
  • Update the master sheet — status, attempts, invoiced, paid
  • Log your miles
  • Build tomorrow's route (go to sheet 1)

6 · Return of Service — Self Audit

Before you sign · every time
A false or defective return is the one mistake you cannot walk back. Run this on every single return.
  • Date AND time the process came to handWhen you received it — the most commonly forgotten field
  • Date AND time it was served, to the minute
  • Manner of service stated — personal, substitute, posting, corporate
  • Full name of the person served, spelled correctly
  • Their capacity if served in a representative role
  • Every pleading and document listed — not "documents," each one named
  • Complete service address including unit number
  • If substitute — all three prongs written out: that it was the usual place of abode, that the person resides there, their age (15+), and that you informed them of the contents
  • If posting an eviction — both attempt times, at least 6 hours apart
  • If unknown occupants — three attempts: business hours, non-business hours, and a weekend, plus that you asked their name
  • Your signature and CPS number
  • Every fact on this return is something you personally did and can swear toIf any line makes you hesitate — don't sign. Re-attempt or call the attorney.
Client app cross-check: before marking a job complete in ProVest, ABC, Proof, or a client portal — confirm the attempt count, photos, and GPS all uploaded and the status matches what your return says. A return that disagrees with the app is the fastest way to lose a network account.

7 · The Weekly Reset

Sunday · 30–45 min
You can't serve on Sunday anyway. Use it.
  • Purge the accordion — everything served and returned moves to the closed box
  • Re-tab what's left by due date, front slot is due this week
  • Chase the money — anything invoiced over 30 days gets one polite email
  • Scan for danger — any serve-by date inside 7 days with zero attempts goes into Monday's prime block right now
  • Review the numbers (sheet 10)
  • Send outreach — new firms, or follow-ups to anyone who hasn't replied
  • Restock the car — paper, water, bug spray, gallon bags, pens
  • Back up the photos — verify the cloud folders actually synced
  • Scout Sunday — drive by tough addresses, photograph tags, confirm occupancy for Monday morning
  • Check renewal dates — certification, bond, notary commission, insurance

16 · Conflict Check

Before accepting any job
You must be a disinterested party. In a county this size, this will come up — catch it on the phone, not after you've served.
  • Do I know either party personally? Plaintiff or defendant
  • Any family connection? Including in-laws and extended family
  • Any financial interest in the outcome, however small?
  • Do my kids know their kids? School, sports, church, neighborhood
  • Have I ever lived at or near this address?
  • Is this connected to any matter I'm personally involved in?
  • Would I be comfortable if opposing counsel asked me all of the above under oath?
Any yes = decline the job. Refer it out — to your mentor or another server. Referring a conflict makes you look professional. Serving one you shouldn't have makes the service challengeable and puts your certification at risk.

Other things I'd think about

Beyond the checklists — these are the things that tend to bite new servers in the first 90 days.

Keep a "hot address" list

One page in the glovebox of every address that went badly — hostile person, dog, gate problem, wrong info. Before any route, check it. Also share it with your mentor; he'll do the same for you, and that trade is how servers keep each other safe.

Your phone is evidence — back it up daily

If your phone dies or gets stolen with three days of un-uploaded attempt photos on it, those serves are challengeable and you have no proof. Auto-upload to the cloud, verify it actually synced during your weekly reset.

Never let a client's app be your only record

If you work for ProVest or ABC and the account ends, you can lose access to your own attempt history. Keep your own copy of every photo, log, and return in your own folders. Your record of your work should never live only on someone else's platform.

Watch for the deadline you didn't notice

A hearing date is not the same as a serve-by date, and some services have to happen a set number of days before an appearance. Ask at intake, write both down, and work backward from the earlier one.

Get comfortable saying no

To a serve outside your circuit. To an enforceable writ. To a case you have any connection to. To a client who won't pay for non-serves. To a serve you're not confident you did correctly. Every one of those is a "no" that protects your certification.

Build the diligent search affidavit as you go

Don't wait until attempt four to start writing it. The observations you record at attempt one are what make it credible — vehicles, occupancy signs, who you spoke to. Written after the fact, it reads thin and an opposing attorney will say so.

Keep one folder called "Proof of Me"

Certification, ID card, bond, insurance, W-9, driver's license, a short bio. Every agency and network asks for the same stack. Build it once and applications take ten minutes instead of an hour.

Ask your mentor for his rejected work

Not just his overflow — the serves he turned down for being too far, too hard, or too small. Those are the ones that build your skills fastest, and they cost him nothing to hand over.

Track revenue per route hour, not per serve

A $65 serve twelve miles away beats a $110 rush forty miles away. Once you have a month of data, you'll see which clients and which zones are actually worth your time — and that's when you start pricing like a business instead of guessing.
