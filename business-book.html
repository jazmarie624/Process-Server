Brand & materials

Your certification makes you legal. Your materials make you look like a business instead of somebody with a car.

Read this before you order anything

Nothing you print, wear, or stick on your car may suggest you are law enforcement. No badges. No shields. No stars. No "OFFICER." No navy-and-gold police styling. Implying you're a cop is a crime in Florida, it voids your service, and it can cost your certification. Your look should read professional courier or legal services — never police. This is the single most common branding mistake new servers make.

The car magnet question — decide on purpose

Why you might

Free advertising in every parking lot, every apartment complex, every courthouse. People do call numbers off cars, and it makes you look established.

Why most working servers don't

A marked car tells a dodger exactly who just pulled up. You lose the element of an ordinary vehicle, which is one of your best tools. It also makes you easier to identify if a serve goes badly.

The compromise: buy removable magnets, not a vinyl wrap. On for networking, courthouse trips, and notary jobs. Off for serving. Never leave them on while working a residential route.

Logo and color use

Black

Backgrounds, nav, headers, anything client-facing that should feel premium.

Gold

Accents, rules, the crown, the CPS number. Never gold text on white — it disappears. Gold belongs on black.

Ivory / white

Your reading surface and your print stock. Warmer than pure white and easier on the eyes.

One typeface pair

A strong serif for your name, a clean sans for everything else. Don't add a third.

Consistency beats cleverness. The same mark, the same gold, the same phone number on every single thing you hand out. Attorneys remember businesses that look the same twice.

Bilingual service

English and Spanish at the door. In Osceola County this is a genuine competitive advantage, not a nice-to-have.

GPS photo proof, every attempt

Not just successful serves. Every attempt, timestamped and located.

Status update the same day

You'll never have to call and ask where your serve is.

What you actually need to open the bank account

Money hygiene from day one

Set aside 25–30%

Every payment, the day it lands, moves to a separate savings bucket. You're 1099 — nobody withholds for you, and quarterly estimated payments may apply once this is real income.

Track mileage automatically

Turn on a mileage app the first day and forget it. Reconstructing a year of driving in April is misery, and mileage is usually your single biggest deduction.

Never mix accounts

One gas fill on the household card and the whole year gets muddy. Business card for business, always.

Services like process serving are generally not subject to Florida sales tax, but confirm with a tax preparer for your specific mix — especially once you add notary and any document services. I'm not a tax professional and this is worth one paid hour with someone who is.

What you're really selling

Attorneys are not buying a delivery. They're buying certainty and quiet.

They need service that survives a motion to quash, and they need to never wonder where it stands. Every single thing in this book — the photo proof, the same-day text, the detailed attempt log, the diligent-search affidavit — exists to sell those two things. Price and speed matter far less than most new servers think.

Who hires process servers

Ranked by how reachable they are for someone brand new. Start at the top and work down.

Tier 1 — go after these first

Eviction & landlord-tenant firmshighest volume

The single best target for a new server. Orange and Osceola both file heavily, the work is recurring weekly, the addresses cluster geographically, and the serves are procedurally repetitive so you get fast at them.

What they care about: speed, the 6-hour posting gap documented correctly, and never having to chase you for a return.

Property management companies & apartment complexesrecurring

Large complexes along 192, in Kissimmee, Poinciana, and Buenaventura Lakes turn over constantly. They route court filings through their attorney, but they hire directly for notice posting — and they always need a reliable person who shows up.

The angle: one property management company with several properties is a steady weekly base. Two of them and your calendar is half full.

Other process servers & local agencieseasiest yes

The fastest first work you'll get. Everyone has overflow, everyone has a week they're underwater, and they'd rather hand it to a certified server than turn it down.

Collections & debt-buyer firms

Very high volume, very repetitive, and they're used to paying per serve on a schedule. Less glamorous, extremely steady.

Tier 2 — reachable once you have a few weeks of history

Family law solos

Divorce, paternity, child support, injunctions. Emotional, evasive defendants and genuinely hard serves — which means they value a server who doesn't give up. Your Spanish matters most here.

Small claims & consumer firms

Steady low-dollar volume. Good filler work between bigger jobs.

Foreclosure firms

High volume, often with multiple defendants at one address, which means multiple fees per stop.

HOA & condo association management

Lien letters, violation notices, association litigation. Central Florida is thick with HOAs.

Insurance defense & subrogation

Witness subpoenas, records subpoenas. Predictable and professional.

Corporate legal departments

Hotels, timeshares, healthcare, large employers. Slow to land, excellent once you're in.

Tier 3 — worth knowing about

Bail bondsmen & private investigators

Adjacent world, overlapping skills, constant referrals in both directions.

Pro se litigants

People filing their own cases need service and don't know who to call. Low volume, pays immediately, no invoicing.

Court reporters

Referral partners, not clients. They're in the same firms you want and they get asked "know a good server?" constantly. Take one to coffee.

Paralegals

The paralegal picks the server far more often than the attorney does. Be nice to every single one.

Title companies

Mostly a notary target rather than a serving target — but the same relationship feeds both.

Immigration & personal injury firms

Heavy Spanish-speaking client bases in this area. Your bilingual pitch lands hard here.

The government path — real jobs, not clients

State Attorney's Office

SAO offices post salaried Process Server positions with state benefits — one recent 11th Circuit posting listed roughly $36,000. Watch the 9th Circuit SAO for the same. You already work for the state, so you know how these postings run.

Sheriff's civil process units

Orange and Osceola both run civil process. They serve the enforceable writs you can't touch. Positions open periodically, and your certification plus state work history is a real résumé.

Court Administration

Honest answer: the court certifies process servers — it doesn't employ them. There's no job to apply for at the courthouse itself. The paid government roles are at the Sheriff and the State Attorney.

Find them — building your prospect list

Cold lists off Google are wasted effort. These methods give you names of firms that are provably buying serves right now.

The docket mining method — do this first

This is the highest-value hour you will spend on marketing.

Both the Osceola and Orange County Clerks of Court have free public case search. Every civil and eviction case has a docket, and on that docket sits a return of service. That document names the process server who did the job and the plaintiff's attorney who ordered it.

  1. Open the clerk's public case search for Osceola County. Filter to recent eviction and county civil cases.
  2. Scan the case list for repeat plaintiffs' attorneys. The same firm names will appear over and over — those are your volume buyers.
  3. Open a docket and find the return of service. Note which server or agency signed it. Now you know who your competition is and who each firm currently uses.
  4. Build a spreadsheet: firm name · attorney · approximate monthly filings · current server · contact info.
  5. Sort by volume. A firm filing 20+ evictions a month is worth ten firms filing two.
  6. Repeat for Orange County. Bigger county, more firms, more volume.
  7. Note the pattern. If one agency signs every return for a firm, that firm is locked. If several different servers appear, that firm spreads work around — and those are your openings.
Why this beats everything else: you're not guessing who might need a server. You're reading a public record of who paid for one last week.

These are starting numbers inside the normal ranges — routine $45–$75, rush $85–$150, same-day $125–$250, non-serve $40–$70. The sheriff's statutory fee anchors the bottom of the market, so don't compete on price. You'll win on speed, communication, and Spanish.

The rules that protect your margin

Always charge for a non-serve

You made the attempts, you drove the miles, you produced an affidavit. Giving that away teaches clients that your failures are free — and it's the most common way new servers lose money.

Never serve a second defendant free

Same address, second person, second fee. It's a second serve and a second return.

Set a free-mileage radius

15–20 miles from St. Cloud. Past that, per-mile. Otherwise a $65 serve in far Orange County costs you money.

Quote add-ons before you start

Skip trace, stakeout, extra attempts. Never surprise a client with a bill — surprise invoices end relationships.

Get the fee in writing

Even a one-line email confirmation. "Confirming $110 rush on Ramirez" is enough.

Raise rates on new clients only

When you're busy, quote higher to the next new firm. Don't raise on the ones who supported you early.

What makes you the easy call

These are your differentiators. Put them on the rack card, in every email, and in your voicemail greeting.

Bilingual at the door

Not "Spanish available" — "I serve in English and Spanish." In Osceola County this wins work over servers with ten years on you.

Photo and GPS on every attempt

Most servers document successful serves. Documenting failed attempts is what makes a diligent search affidavit survive a challenge — and attorneys notice immediately.

Same-day status, unprompted

One text: served, time, return tonight. Attorneys forgive slow serves. They never forgive silence.

Early morning and weekend attempts

Many servers only work business hours. Saying "I run 6 AM and Saturday attempts" tells a firm you'll get their hard ones.

Notary included

Once commissioned, "notarization included at no extra charge with any serve" is a real, concrete reason to pick you.

Be the 4 PM Friday person

Whoever answers a rush at 4 PM on a Friday owns that firm's business. Availability beats price every time.

Contract work — getting hired by other companies

The fastest way to fill your calendar while you build your own book. Lower pay per serve, but no selling and no invoicing chase.

Why do this even though you want your own clients
  • Volume from day one, so your route is never half empty
  • Their apps enforce photo, GPS, and attempt standards — you learn good habits for free
  • Steady 1099 income while your own pipeline is still cold
  • You still serve under your certification, so every serve builds your record
Read the contract before you sign — every time
  • Non-solicit clauses. Many agency agreements bar you from soliciting their clients. Sign with a local agency, then pitch the same eviction firm, and you have a problem. Know exactly who's off-limits.
  • Payment terms. Net 30 is common, net 45 happens.
  • Who eats the non-serve? Some pay only on successful service — five attempts for $0 is a real risk.
  • Chargebacks if a serve gets quashed.
  • Insurance requirements beyond your bond.
  • Exclusivity — make sure you can still work for yourself.

Your application pack — build once, reuse forever

The pay trade, honestly

Network work

Less per serve, sometimes noticeably less. But zero sales time, zero invoicing chase, zero marketing spend.

Your own clients

Full rate, you keep it all. But hours of prospecting, invoicing, and chasing payment.

The right mix

Network jobs fill empty slots in routes you're already driving. Your own clients fill the prime windows. Never drive a route for network work alone.

Reported ranges run roughly $25–$75 per successful service, with independent servers landing somewhere around $1,000–$2,500 a week at real volume. Treat published averages loosely — they mix part-timers, employees, and agency owners together.

The notary stack

A notary commission is the highest-return add-on for a process server. It's cheap, it's fast, and it turns one relationship into two revenue streams.

The limit you have to know first

You cannot notarize your own signature, and you cannot notarize a document you're a party to. So a notary commission does not let you notarize your own returns of service. Anyone who tells you otherwise is wrong, and doing it would be misconduct on both licenses.

Ask your mentor whether the firms you serve for even require notarized returns — many Florida returns are executed under penalty of perjury rather than notarized.

Why it pairs so well with serving

Same customers

The firms and property managers who buy serves also need notarizations constantly. You're already in their building.

Same trip

You're already driving to a courthouse, a firm, an apartment office. A notarization on the same trip is nearly pure margin.

A reason to be picked

"Notarization included with any serve" is a concrete, specific differentiator that competitors can't match without going and getting commissioned.

Income that doesn't require a door

Notary work happens in offices, hospitals, and homes by appointment. Different rhythm than serving, and it fills your midday hours.

Bilingual again

A bilingual mobile notary in Osceola County is genuinely in demand, especially for title and real estate work.

It leads somewhere

Notary is the doorway to loan signing agent work, which pays multiples of a standard notarization.

Getting commissioned in Florida

RequirementDetail
Basic eligibility18 or older, Florida resident, able to read and write English, no disqualifying felony conviction without restored civil rights.
EducationA required notary education course for first-time applicants. Usually 3 hours, often bundled free with a bonding agency package.
BondA $7,500 surety bond is required for Florida notaries — separate from your $5,000 process server bond.
TermFour years.
CostMost people go through a bonding agency that bundles the state fee, bond, course, stamp, and journal into one package. Budget roughly $100–$150 all in, and compare two or three agencies.
SuppliesOfficial stamp (from a bonded vendor, not a print shop), a journal, and ideally an embosser. Keep the journal religiously — it's your protection.
Verify current fees, bond amount, and course requirements with the Florida Department of State before you pay anyone. These change, and bonding agencies aren't always current on their marketing pages.

What you can charge

Florida caps notary fees by statute

The maximum fee per notarial act is set in law and it's low — roughly $10 per act, with a separate higher fee allowed for solemnizing a marriage. Look up the current statutory maximum and don't exceed it.

Where the actual money is: a separately disclosed travel fee, agreed in advance with the signer. That's how mobile notaries make it worthwhile — the notarial act is capped, the travel is not. Disclose it clearly and in advance, every time.

Loan signing agent — where notary income gets serious

What it is

A notary who's specifically trained to walk borrowers through a real estate loan package. Signings commonly pay in the range of $75–$200 each, and one signing can take 60–90 minutes.

This connects directly to the MLO track you're already on — same industry, same vocabulary, same people.

What it takes
  • Active Florida notary commission
  • Loan signing certification and a background screening (the National Notary Association is the common route)
  • A dual-tray laser printer — loan packages print on both letter and legal. This is the real startup cost.
  • Errors & omissions insurance, usually required by signing services
  • Registration with signing services and title companies
Remote Online Notarization (RON) is also available in Florida and lets you notarize by video. It requires separate registration, an approved technology provider, and an additional bond. Worth exploring once your standard commission is running, since it's income you can earn from your kitchen table.

The full stack you're building

Process server → notary → loan signing agent → MLO

Each one feeds the next. Serving gets you inside law firms and property management offices. Notary makes you useful to those same people on days there's nothing to serve. Loan signing puts you in title companies and mortgage offices. And that's the exact room where the MLO license pays off.

That's not four side hustles. It's one career with four doors into the same industry.
